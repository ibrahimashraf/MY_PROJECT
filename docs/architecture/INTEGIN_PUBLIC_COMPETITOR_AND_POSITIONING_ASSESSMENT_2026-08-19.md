# INTEGIN Public Competitor and Positioning Assessment

**Date:** 2026-08-19  
**Scope:** Public website claims only for Daarsoft, Spinnsol/Zertify, QwikSpec, and Bexel, compared with INTEGIN's documented future direction.  
**Confidence:** Medium. Public sites were reviewed; no product was trialled, no customer was contacted, no contract was negotiated, and no private technical system was accessed.  
**Important status boundary:** Competitors are compared as publicly marketed products. INTEGIN capabilities described as future/roadmap remain **unimplemented design intent** unless separately evidenced as shipped.

## Executive assessment

The four companies are credible adjacent comparators, but they address different centres of gravity. Daarsoft markets the broadest publicly priced all-in-one third-party inspection suite; Spinnsol/Zertify markets TIC vertical depth with lifting/NDT/CCU and offline/own-server language; QwikSpec is strongest in construction/project-site workflow, real-time operations, timesheets, and location-aware field management; and Bexel combines oil-and-gas/TIC operations, quote-to-order flow, training, client access, verification, and ERP/Zoho integration. [1] [2] [3] [4]

INTEGIN should **not** compete by saying that competitors lack QR, offline, certificates, assets, training, dashboards, or invoicing: public claims show these are common category features. The defensible opportunity is a more explicit operating model: controlled work-order scope and change handling; expandable-location field work; safe fully-editable cloning without proof reuse; partial submission, handover, and reconciliation; separately governed certificate and competence lifecycles; privacy-bounded verification; and strict commercial-to-technical non-interference. These are currently product-design differentiators, not public performance claims.

The supplied private-sovereign reference is strategically useful as a **positioning direction**, especially for inspection bodies requiring customer-controlled/private deployment. However, Daarsoft publicly lists an on-premise enterprise option and Spinnsol publicly uses Azure-or-own-server language; INTEGIN must therefore explain its eventual deployment, data-control, operational-support, and assurance boundaries precisely rather than claim uniqueness. [5] [2]

## Strengths INTEGIN should respect

The research does not support treating any competitor as a simple or weak alternative. Daarsoft presents a comprehensive, immediately understandable package and price anchor for smaller teams. Spinnsol/Zertify presents vertical credibility for lifting, NDT, and TIC workflows, including tags and offline use. QwikSpec presents a credible field-first story around location, operations, timesheets, escalations, and construction delivery. Bexel presents a coherent oil-and-gas/TIC workflow from commercial quotation through order, assignment, verification, portal access, and integration. [1] [2] [3] [4]

These market signals mean INTEGIN must make common features operationally excellent before treating them as differentiation: mobile/offline field work, asset registers, templates, certificates, QR verification, expiry notifications, client access, dashboards, and invoicing all appear in public competitor positioning.

## Defensible INTEGIN direction

| INTEGIN direction | Why it matters | Positioning rule today |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Work order → expandable location → independent record model** | Matches how inspectors organise mixed sites while retaining asset/person/site record integrity. | Present as planned workflow design until implemented and field-tested. |
| **Safe clone with editable draft data, never copied proof** | Lets high-volume field work remain fast without making copied photos/signatures/receipts look like new evidence. | Explain as an assurance-oriented workflow choice, not a claim that competitors copy proof. |
| **Partial submission, handover, explicit reconciliation** | Supports multiple inspectors and work across days without falsely closing the whole order. | Validate in a field pilot before marketing as a proven advantage. |
| **Certificate, competence, commercial separation** | Protects technical authority from training, payment, invoicing, and administrative state. | Describe the rationale; do not claim it is legally mandatory or universally superior. |
| **Read-only verification portal** | Separates public verification from authoring systems and supports current/revoked/superseded status. | Do not say QR/portal guarantees regulator or third-party acceptance. |
| **Private/sovereign deployment posture** | Relevant for regulated/private inspection bodies and customer-controlled environments. | Define the eventual deployment/support/data-residency boundaries; do not call it unique. |
| **Lawful regulator/ZATCA adapter boundaries** | Prepares integrations without letting external/commercial state mutate technical truth. | Never claim integration, approval, or compliance before official onboarding/testing. |

## What INTEGIN is currently missing relative to visible market surface

The future direction is broad, but it does not yet provide an implemented public market offer. The largest practical gaps are therefore execution and proof: production-grade mobile/field workflow, complete work-order/asset/certificate/competence data model, client-facing portal, publicly supportable deployment options, document/template output engine, visible commercial package, published legal/data-governance terms, validated offline reconciliation, and implementation evidence for the planned verification and integration boundaries.

The fastest competitive priority is not every feature at once. It is a credible **core inspection operating system**: authoritative work orders, location-organised field execution, independent receipted records, high-volume clone/partial-submit flow, review/certificate path, and clear completion/timesheet reporting. Customer portal, renewal, support, training, verification, integrations, and invoicing should follow in their documented dependency order.

## Public pricing and policy observations

Daarsoft is the only reviewed competitor with clear public numeric tiers, which gives buyers an immediate self-service anchor. Its published entry and growing-team plans should be used as a market reference, not copied as an INTEGIN price recommendation; INTEGIN’s private/sovereign, support, deployment, and implementation model may create a materially different cost structure. [5]

Spinnsol/Zertify, QwikSpec, and Bexel are contact-led or non-numeric in the reviewed public materials. QwikSpec’s public construction page lists licensing approaches but no numerical price, while Bexel’s public pricing page did not expose pricing in the reviewed text. [9] [13] This is evidence of public pricing visibility only, not evidence of actual cost.

Public policy maturity also varies. Daarsoft has clear high-level terms including data ownership and QR acceptance disclaimers; QwikSpec publishes both website and Field privacy text, including location collection when enabled; Bexel’s reviewed terms are generic website content; and a detailed Spinnsol official policy was not found in the reviewed material. [7] [10] [12] The absence or genericity of reviewed public policy text must not be interpreted as an absence of contractual controls.

## Technology and deployment findings

Only limited public signals are available. Spinnsol describes cloud/SaaS and Azure-or-own-server database language; Daarsoft lists on-premise in its enterprise offering; QwikSpec’s public Field policy documents GPS/location collection; Bexel publicly claims ERP/Zoho integration. [2] [5] [10] [11]

Public marketing-site technology is not product architecture. Bexel’s public page metadata/assets indicated WordPress/Elementor and marketing tools, but this provides no valid conclusion about Bexel's product backend, hosting, data isolation, security, or reliability. The same restraint applies to all competitors.

## Recommended market position

INTEGIN should position itself as:

> **A controlled inspection operating system for high-volume, multi-location, multi-inspector work—designed for traceable field execution, governed certificate/competence lifecycles, and deployment/integration boundaries that customers can understand.**

The message should lead with operational outcomes, not internal architecture jargon:

1. **Inspect faster without losing record integrity:** expandable locations, safe clone, partial submission, and exception-led queues.
2. **Control complex shared jobs:** each inspector closes their assigned work; the office confirms the combined completion basis.
3. **Keep technical trust separate from sales and finance:** work, certificates, training, support, invoicing, and future external adapters have explicit roles and status.
4. **Verify honestly:** future QR/reference verification indicates current internal status and never pretends external acceptance.
5. **Deploy with clarity:** offer public SaaS, private hosted, or customer-controlled deployment only after each is technically and commercially defined.

## Claims INTEGIN must avoid

INTEGIN must not say that competitors lack features because their public pages do not mention them. It must not say that its planned designs are already shipped or production-proven. It must not claim unique private/sovereign deployment, security, tenancy isolation, regulatory acceptance, ZATCA compatibility, fraud prevention, QR acceptance, cost advantage, uptime, or standards compliance without implementation evidence and, where applicable, official validation.

## Evidence-led next steps

1. Build a public feature/dependency matrix that labels every INTEGIN capability as **shipped**, **pilot**, **designed**, or **future**.
2. Validate the highest-value field flows with pilot users: partial submission, cloned defective records, location expansion, handover, and correction/reconciliation.
3. Publish concise customer-facing data/deployment/retention and certificate-verification boundary notes before using private/sovereign positioning.
4. Prototype the read-only verification experience using only synthetic sample records and test it with clients/site gatekeepers.
5. Design public commercial packaging after delivery/deployment costs are known; do not choose a price solely from competitor pages.
6. Validate any future SASO/SAAC/ZATCA or other external adapter with official onboarding and subject-matter/legal review before positioning it as a supported integration.
